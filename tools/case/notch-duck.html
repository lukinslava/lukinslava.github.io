A home for the notch

The notch is the one part of the screen that is always there and never used. Here it becomes a small home: closed, it is the ordinary black notch; on hover it grows into a panel; when something happens, a character steps out and says what is going on.

One character, every state

Instead of spinners and toasts, the app talks through one duck. Each state has its own drawing and one short line, and the result names exactly what happened — how many files moved and where.

Hello“Quack! I'm here” — on launch.
Noticing“Whoa, what a mess… let's tidy up.”
Working“Working…” — while files move.
Done“Done! Moved 9 files to the 2026-10-11 folder.”
Goodbye“Bye-bye, amigo!” — on quit.

The duck is my own drawing.

Hover the notch

The panel drops out of the notch itself, black like the notch, so it reads as the notch growing rather than a window opening. Three tabs: what was just copied, what was just captured and the links that get opened every day.

Tidy the desktop

Screenshots, downloads and drafts pile up on the desktop. One click sweeps them all into a folder named with today's date, so nothing is deleted and everything is easy to find by day.

Claude is waiting

A long Claude Code task runs in the background while you do something else. When Claude stops to ask a question, the notch lights up — a spot at the top of the screen that is always in view.
