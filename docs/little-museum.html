Little Museum

A home app for the drawings and crafts of two brothers. A parent photographs a sheet on the table, the app lifts the artwork off the wood and hangs it in a museum — with its artist, its date and the sentence the artist said about it.

The problem

Kids draw and make things every day. Paper gets lost, crafts break, and photos in the camera roll get buried among everything else. The family needed one place where every piece is kept neatly — with its artist and date — and is a pleasure to look back on.

Three ways in

Everything a child makes falls into one of three shapes, and each one is captured differently.

01

A drawing on a sheet

A parent photographs the sheet on the table. The app finds the paper's edges, corrects the perspective and hangs the drawing in a frame.

02

A figure cut with scissors

The app separates the figure from the table along its outline and saves it as a PNG with a transparent background.

03

A craft

Photos from four sides. The app is ready to build a 3D model from them, so a paper rocket can be turned around on screen.

Cutout without neural networks

The background is removed right on the phone — no servers, no ML models. The trick is that a wooden table has only a few colours, and a child's figure almost never shares them.

01

Table colours

k-means over the pixels along the frame edges picks out a few table colours — the light and dark grain of the wood. Objects that touch the edge, like a stack of paper, are ignored.

02

Background fill

The background spreads inward from the edges through similar colours until it meets a sharp boundary — the edge of the paper. That is why skin, close in hue to light wood, survives.

03

Outline

The largest region is kept; fuzz along the edge and splinters of table are dropped.

04

Result

The mask is smoothed and applied to the full-size photo — a PNG with transparency, ready to hang.

A sheet is a different problem

If the photo holds a rectangular sheet rather than a figure, the app switches to “sheet” mode on its own: it checks how well the shape it found matches a quadrilateral through its corners, straightens the perspective and trims the edges. Both modes can be switched by hand, and the cutout has a “gentler / bolder” slider.

Hanging a piece

Scanning had to survive a parent with one free hand: pick what kind of piece it is, take the photo, check the corners, nudge the cutout if needed, name the artist. Everything else the app does itself.

Screens

Five places: the museum wall, collections, scanning, the story and settings. Frames — wood, tape, pin, mat — are picked from the artwork's id, so a piece always comes back in the frame you remember it in.

Design

The app should feel like a small gallery, not a photo folder. A bookish serif for the taglines and the titles of works, a quiet geometric sans for the interface, one green accent and six pastel tints for collections and children. The reference was album apps with pocket cards, and frames hung on a wall.

Type

Literata for taglines and artwork titles — a bookish, gallery feel. Unbounded at light weights for the interface.

Colour

One green accent, #417B5A, and six pastel tints that mark collections and tell the two artists apart.

Frames

Wood, tape, pin and mat. The frame comes from the artwork's id, so the wall looks hung by hand rather than generated.

In the hand

How it is built

App
React, TypeScript, Vite. A progressive web app: it installs on the home screen and opens without a browser bar.
Data
Supabase — database and private file storage, with access limited to the family's emails through row-level security.
3D
An Edge Function talks to Meshy; model-viewer shows the result and puts a paper rocket on the floor in AR.
Hosting
GitHub Pages. No backend of my own to keep alive.

A family project. The drawings and the plasticine caterpillar on the screens were generated for this demo; the paper superhero is the one real piece.
